## Where ours disagreed with the baseline

2 of 22 questions. This is the list to mine for the next round of training data.

**q10 — Above what amount do expense claims require a receipt?**

- baseline: faith 1.0 · rel 1.0 · cite ok
- ours: faith 1.0 · rel 0.0 · cite ok
- judge said: _Both factual claims are directly supported by source [1]: the 500 rupees threshold for receipts and the acceptability of photographs of paper receipts._
- our answer: `Expenses over **500 rupees** require receipts [1]. Photographs of paper receipts are acceptable [1].`

**q20 — Who owns work produced for a client?**

- baseline: faith 1.0 · rel 1.0 · cite ok
- ours: faith 1.0 · rel 0.5 · cite ok
- judge said: _The answer is directly supported by passages [1] and [2], which both state this exact fact about client work ownership upon final payment._
- our answer: `Work produced for a client belongs to that client on final payment [1][2].`

## Reading this honestly

A 3B model quantized to 4 bits and served on a CPU is not supposed to
beat Claude, and did not need to. What it had to show is that the
*trained behaviour* transferred: cite real passages, refuse when the
answer isn't there. Those two rows are the result. Faithfulness and
latency are the price, and both were known before we started —
production stays on Claude for exactly that reason.

## Follow-up: is the relevance gap real?

`relevance` is the only metric where the two models differ (0.977 vs 0.909).
It is worth 3 questions out of 22, so it deserved a look rather than a shrug.

### q10 — the one that scored 0.0

| | answer | relevance |
|---|---|---:|
| Claude | "Receipts are required for every expense claim over **500 rupees** [1]." | 1.0 |
| Ours | "Expenses over **500 rupees** require receipts [1]." | 0.0 |

Same fact, same citation, same figure. So we re-ran the judge on the identical
text several times:

```
ours   (8 samples)   7 x 0.0,  1 x 1.0
Claude (5 samples)   4 x 1.0,  1 x 0.0
ours, markdown stripped (3 samples)   3 x 1.0
ours, reworded to "Expense claims over..."  no change
```

**The judge is not deterministic.** `evaluation.relevance()` is a single haiku
call per question, at default temperature, with no repeats — so one borderline
answer moves the aggregate by 1/22 = 0.045. That is two thirds of the entire
0.068 gap, sitting on a coin-flip.

The judge's own stated reason for the 0.0 was *"without access to the source
documents, this cannot be verified"* — which is not a relevance objection at
all, and applies equally to Claude's answer. The relevance judge is never given
sources; that is by design (`faithfulness` grades grounding, `relevance` grades
whether the question was answered).

### q20 — this one IS a real difference

| | answer |
|---|---|
| Claude | "**The client owns work produced for them upon final payment.** [1][2] However, tools, libraries and internal components the studio builds during the project remain the studio's property." |
| Ours | "Work produced for a client belongs to that client on final payment [1][2]." |

Ours is correct but terser — it stops at the direct answer and drops the
carve-out. Claude volunteers the nuance. 0.5 vs 1.0 is a fair call here, and it
is the clearest genuine quality difference in the whole run: **the 3B model
answers the question asked and little more.**

### What this changes

- The headline gap of **-0.068 relevance** is roughly **-0.023 real** (q20's
  terseness) and **-0.045 measurement noise** (q10's unstable judge).
- Faithfulness, citation validity, retrieval hit and refusal-on-traps are all
  **exactly tied** — and those are the metrics the fine-tune was actually
  trained to move.
- The eval harness has a bug worth fixing: relevance should be sampled more
  than once, or run at temperature 0. That is a finding about our own tooling,
  discovered because the numbers were interrogated instead of published.

### Caveat on the traps

Only **2 of 22** golden questions are refusal traps, so "2/2 vs 2/2" is a weak
signal — a single question moves it 50 points. Both models refused both traps,
and the fine-tuned model reproduced the refusal sentence character-for-character,
but this row deserves a bigger trap set before anyone leans on it.
